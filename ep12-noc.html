01The business in thirty seconds

Data as of 31 Dec 2025 · FY2025 10-K (Item 1 and sales disclosures)

What it does

Describes itself as "a leading global aerospace and defense technology company". Four reportable segments: Aeronautics Systems, Defense Systems, Mission Systems and Space Systems. Approximately 95,000 employees at 31 December 2025.

Who pays

The US government accounted for 84% of 2025 sales, international customers 14% and other customers 2%.

Where it sells

By region, 2025 sales were $35,964m in the United States (86%), $3,286m in Europe (8%), $1,919m in Asia/Pacific (5%) and $785m in other regions (2%), a total of $41,954m.

How it is paid

Half of 2025 sales came from fixed-price contracts and half from cost-reimbursable contracts. Section 03 shows the split over three years and what the company says about the risk.

The 10-K says that "Fixed-price contracts tend to have more financial risk than cost-type contracts", and that if the company does not achieve its estimates or meet contract terms, its profitability has at times been, and may be, reduced, and that it has incurred and may incur losses. In practice: on a fixed-price contract the price is set in advance, so cost overruns fall on the company; on a cost-type contract allowed costs are reimbursed plus a fee.

The base year is not clean

Q2 2025 includes a $231m gain on the sale of the training services business, recorded within operating income. The release states that Q2 2025 net earnings include a benefit of $150m, or $1.04 per diluted share, from the divestiture. The comparison of margin and EPS above therefore mixes a normal quarter with a quarter that had a one-off gain.

What moved the two weaker margins

  • Defense Systems: the company attributes the decline primarily to lower net EAC (estimate at completion) adjustments, including the absence of a $76m favorable adjustment on Sentinel in the prior-year quarter. A favorable Sentinel adjustment tied to contract incentives partly offset higher AARGM-ER and SiAW costs. A $68m unfavorable adjustment on SiAW reflects costs to develop and qualify the weapon.
  • Space Systems: the company attributes the decline primarily to lower net EAC adjustments, including a $91m unfavorable adjustment on GEM 63XL, largely from a higher projected cost to complete the program, partly offset by favorable contract mix.

2. The program charges

Two programs needed higher cost estimates in Q2: $68m on SiAW (Defense Systems) and $91m on GEM 63XL (Space Systems). The question for the next report is whether the company reports further adjustments on either program.

3. The margins that fell

Defense Systems was at 7.5% against 12.7% a year earlier, and Space Systems at 8.6% against 10.6%. The full-year Space outlook is "low 10%". Defense was affected by the absence of a $76m favorable Sentinel adjustment in the prior-year quarter.

4. The outlook

Whether the 2026 outlook for sales and EPS (raised in July), operating income and free cash flow (reaffirmed) is reaffirmed, raised or cut, and whether the segment margin outlooks move again.

5. Cash and backlog

Adjusted free cash flow was $978m in Q2 against a 2026 outlook of $3.10–3.50bn. Backlog was $104.7bn at 30 June 2026.

07The Today Test, in this hypothetical case

If this were a position already held, the question that matters comes after the report: would you buy this business again today, knowing everything you now know, blind to what you paid? The Today Test does not run before a report. It runs on what the report reveals. Until the number arrives, the checkpoint can only say what to read, not what it will mean.
