01What it does, how it makes money

Agnico Eagle runs ten producing gold mines. That's the entire business model in one sentence: dig up gold, sell it at the market price. There's no product to differentiate, no brand, no pricing power. Revenue is ounces produced, times the market price of gold. Silver, copper and zinc come out of the same ground as byproducts — they lower the net cost, they're not a second business.

02The moat: cost, not brand

In a business that sells an undifferentiated commodity at a price nobody controls, there's exactly one competitive advantage available: producing it cheaper than the next miner. That advantage has a name — all-in sustaining cost (AISC) per ounce.

Agnico's cost advantage over its nearest large peer is $162/oz. Over the most expensive of the four, $362/oz. Multiplied by roughly 3.4 million ounces a year, that gap alone is worth somewhere between $550M and $1.2B of extra margin — every year, just for running better mines.

04The reservation — why not top-tier

A deep dive judges where a business is heading, not just where it stands today. That's where the gap opens up.

Gold is up 54% this year. Quarterly EBITDA grew 43%. Free cash flow — the cash that actually reaches shareholders — grew just 2.3%. A 41-point gap, in a single quarter, between a metal rally and the cash it produced.

The reason is on the record: capital spending guidance was raised 18% this year, largely for a new mine that only starts producing after 2030. And near-term production isn't growing — it's shrinking. A rock movement at one open pit is cutting output by up to 150,000 ounces a year, in both 2027 and 2028.

The growth story is real, funded and credible. It simply lands almost entirely outside the 24-month window this kind of analysis is built to judge.

05Signals worth naming

✗

Company insiders — including the CEO — sold shares in the trailing 90 days. Nobody bought.

✗

Two fatal accidents occurred at two different operations within two months, triggering a company-wide safety review.

✗

One long-running, low-severity lawsuit — a neighborhood nuisance claim near one mine, active since 2017, not material at this company's scale.

Neither the insider activity nor the safety incidents sink the business. Together, they're part of the difference between calling something flawless and calling it very good.

06Verdict

🟡 Good Business — not top-tier

Not because of one disqualifying flaw, but because trajectory counts as much as position: rising costs, falling near-term production, cash flow that didn't follow the metal higher, insider selling with nobody on the other side of the trade.

Each item, alone, is a footnote. Together, they're the exact gap between the best business in an industry and a flawless one. This is a business judgment only — it says nothing about whether today's price offers any margin of safety. That question belongs to a separate stage of this process.
