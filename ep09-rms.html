01The business in thirty seconds

Data as of 29 Jul 2026 · FY2025 universal registration document

What it does

Designs, manufactures — mostly in France, largely by hand — and sells luxury objects: leather bags and saddlery, clothing and accessories, silk, jewellery, homeware, watches and perfume.

How it makes money

Selling at full price through its own network: 234 of its 294 exclusive stores are company-operated and generate 92% of retail revenue, alongside its own e-commerce. No discount sales.

Products that matter

Leather Goods & Saddlery is 46% of revenue. Ready-to-wear & Accessories is 27%. Everything else — silk, jewellery, home, watches, perfume, other brands — is the remaining 27%.

Where it operates

45 countries. Asia-Pacific excluding Japan is 43% of revenue, the Americas 19%, Europe excluding France 14%, Japan 10%, France 9%, Middle East and other 4%.

02How it got here

Data as of 12 Feb 2026 (FY2025) and 29 Jul 2026 (H1 2026)

Hermès was founded in Paris in 1837. Its saddlery origins survive in the name of its largest division, Leather Goods & Saddlery. It is still controlled by the founding family through an unusual legal form, a partnership limited by shares (société en commandite par actions): the general partner, Émile Hermès SAS, is family-owned and by statute receives 0.67% of distributable profits.

Over the last five years revenue went from €9.0 billion to €16.0 billion. The pace of growth, measured at constant exchange rates, has come down every year since 2022:

Revenue growth at constant exchange rates, as reported by the company. 2021 was +42%, a post-pandemic rebound. At current exchange rates, H1 2026 grew +1.6%: currency movements cut more than €360 million from reported revenue in the half.

05Two engines, running at different speeds

Data as of 29 Jul 2026 · growth at constant exchange rates, H1 2026

Accelerating to +10.2% in Q2. Volume is capped by workshop capacity; the company says what it produces is sold.

Everything else · 54%

≈ +3%
Ready-to-wear, silk, jewellery, home, watches, perfume and other brands, taken together.

The group grew by about €490 million at constant exchange rates in the half. About €351 million of that came from leather goods. The other divisions, on a base of about €4.5 billion, grew roughly 3%. Weighted: 0.46 × ~10% + 0.54 × ~3% ≈ 6% — which is the group's reported growth.

The leather / non-leather split is calculated from the company's reported divisional figures; Hermès does not publish it in this form.

Price and volume. For leather goods in 2026, management described the mix as price increases of a "mid-single digit" plus roughly 6% volume. On the H1 results call, the CFO said price increases in 2027 will be "slightly lower" than in 2026.

06The moat — limited by people and leather, not by money

Data as of 29 Jul 2026 · H1 press release and results call

  • Hand-made capacity. Management said on the H1 call that a bag takes around 15 hours of hand-stitching. Craftspeople are trained in-house.
  • One new workshop a year. 25 leather workshops in France; the 25th opened in Loupes in April 2026. Next: Charleville-Mézières (2027), Colombelles (2028), Les Andelys (by 2030).
  • Raw material. Management names the quality of hides as the bottleneck; the group owns tanneries to secure it.
  • Demand above supply. "Everything we produce has been sold" — management, H1 2026 call.
  • Own distribution, full price. 92% of retail revenue through company-operated stores; no sales periods.
  • Gross margin 71.1% in H1 2026, with about half of its costs incurred in France.

Two things are visible. Against the largest leather-goods competitors (LVMH's Fashion & Leather Goods, which includes Louis Vuitton and Dior, and Gucci), Hermès grew while they shrank, and kept a higher margin. The companies growing faster than Hermès sell something else — Richemont's jewellery (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels), Cucinelli's knitwear, Moncler's outerwear — and all of them report lower margins.

The current tax liability rose from €452 million to €787 million in six months, because the 2025 portion of France's exceptional corporate tax contribution was booked in the half and is paid later. It is a timing effect that reverses. The inventory line consumed more cash than a year earlier, not less.

Gross inventory rose 22.7% in 2024 against revenue growth of 13.0%, and 29.6% since the end of 2023 against 20.1% for trailing revenue. The share of inventory written down went from about a quarter to more than a third. Inventory valuation is a key audit matter in both years; the company does not explain the rise in the provision.

3 · The tax rate went up, not the business costs

Effective tax rate: 28.7% (2024) → 33.4% (2025) → 35.4% (H1 2026). The whole difference is France's exceptional contribution (41.2% surcharge on corporate income tax), about €360 million in 2026, of which €270 million in the first half. Without it, 2025 net profit would have grown about 5% instead of falling 1.7%.

10Capital — shares, buybacks, dividends

Data as of 30 Jun 2026

  • Share count unchanged: 105,569,412 shares issued at the end of 2024, 2025 and June 2026. No dilution, no shrinkage.
  • Buybacks are for employees: €160 million in H1 2026, allocated to employee share plans. They offset the plans; they do not reduce the share count. Share-based pay: €132 million in 2025 (0.8% of revenue); free shares go to all employees.
  • Dividends: €18 per share on 2025 (€1,924 million paid in H1 2026). The dividend on 2024 included a €10 exceptional amount.
  • Cash retained: €12.9 billion of restated net cash, no meaningful debt.
  • Insider filings (AMF): members of the Dumas family, including one member of the Supervisory Board, filed share purchases between June and September 2026. No insider sales filed since 16 September 2025.

11Business risks

Status as of 25 Sep 2026

Demand

The slowdown is in the largest region and in more than half of the product range

Growth at constant exchange rates has fallen from 21% (2023) to 6.1% (H1 2026). Asia-Pacific excluding Japan, 43% of revenue, grew 2.4%. The non-leather divisions, 54% of revenue, grew about 3%. Third-party data for July 2026 shows Hermès sales in China declining. Management says purchases by its clients follow real-estate and equity markets more than GDP.

Pricing

Smaller price increases in 2027

Management has said 2027 price increases will be slightly lower than 2026. Price is one of the two drivers of leather growth; the other, volume, is capped by capacity.

Inventory

Stock growing faster than sales, with a rising write-down share

Provision up from 26.2% to 36.2% of gross inventory since the end of 2023, without explanation from the company.

Tax

France's "exceptional" contribution keeps being renewed

Introduced for 2025, renewed for 2026 at the same rate, and confirmed by the finance minister on 24 Aug 2026 for the 2027 budget, "preferably" at a lower rate. About €360 million in 2026, roughly 7% of profit. In its own risk map, Hermès raised tax risk from "possible" to "very possible".

Currency & trade

Reported numbers exposed to exchange rates and tariffs

More than €360 million of revenue lost to currency in H1 2026; hedging costs of about €210 million for the year. US tariffs on EU goods: sources differ between a 15% all-in ceiling (European Commission) and 10% under Section 301 since 24 Jul 2026. Americas revenue grew 15.3% in H1.

Regional

Middle East

Revenue down 4.2% in H1 2026, which management said took about 1.5 points off group growth, with gradual recovery in Q2.

Litigation

US antitrust case on Birkin bag sales

The class action (Cavalleri et al., N.D. Cal. 3:24-cv-01707), alleging Birkin bags were sold only to clients who bought other items, was dismissed with prejudice on 17 Sep 2025 after a third attempt. The appeal (9th Circuit, No. 25-6587) is fully briefed, with no oral argument scheduled and no decision as of 25 Sep 2026. Hermès reports no proceedings with a significant impact.

Governance

Family control and related parties

Partnership limited by shares, controlled by the family. Related-party contracts are disclosed and small relative to revenue — e.g. store design studio RDAI (€20 million in 2025) and leases on rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré — but awarded to family-linked entities without tender.

What changed in the company's own risk disclosure (FY2025 vs FY2024 universal registration document): tax risk and image & reputation risk moved up from "possible" to "very possible"; fraud impact moved up from "limited" to "significant". A sentence stating that a severe external crisis "would be even more unfavourable" in Asia was removed — in the year Asia-Pacific excluding Japan became the slowest region.
