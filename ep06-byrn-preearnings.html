02The habit that broke

This process runs cold before an earnings report — no opinion, just pattern. And here, the loudest signal wasn't a headline. It was a habit the company broke.

Byrna has a track record of pre-announcing revenue weeks ahead of its official report — it had done exactly that for two straight quarters. This time, thirty-nine days after the quarter closed, that number never came. In its place: five unrelated announcements about marketing and personnel — nothing about sales.

An absence, read as a signal, is still evidence — the pattern was two-for-two before this quarter went silent.

03What was already on the record

That silence followed something the company had already put in writing. Three months earlier, on the previous earnings call, management said the coming quarter was — its own words —

“developing below our expectations,” CEO, prior earnings call — guidance pulled, three months before this report

and pulled formal guidance because it didn't have enough internal visibility to give one.

04The reaction history

The market's reaction to this stock's last four reports has been violent in both directions — swings from roughly minus thirty-one percent to plus twenty-one percent in a single session, with nothing calm in between.

One of those swings is the sharpest warning of the four: a quarter with revenue up over forty percent year over year and earnings beating estimates by nearly sixty percent — and it still closed down twenty-one percent on the day. In this stock, a good number has not historically been paid for.

05The checklist

Not a prediction — a checklist

None of this says what the number will be. It's a checklist, and by the day before the report, every box on it was checked in the same direction: a broken pre-announcement habit, a guidance pull already on the record, and a reaction history that has never rewarded a good print in this name.

The pre-earnings checkpoint doesn't forecast an outcome — it establishes what was already knowable going in, so the post-earnings read can be judged against it, not against hindsight.
